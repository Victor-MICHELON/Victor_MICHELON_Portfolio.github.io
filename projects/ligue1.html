Course project with Maël Paget · May 2026

A whole Ligue 1 season predicted before kick-off

Predict the outcome of every match of the 2025-2026 Ligue 1 season (home win, draw or away win) in August, before a ball is kicked. With Maël Paget, we built a pipeline that rebuilds each feature as it was known before the match, from squad values, club hierarchy, recent form, head-to-head records and in-game events, then compared three models. The best one reaches 48.0% accuracy in cross-validation, against 34.1% for a random baseline.

  • 48.0%cross-validated accuracy of the gradient boosting model, on three possible outcomes
  • +14 ptsover a baseline that guesses at random with the historical class frequencies (34.1%)
  • 4,691historical matches used for training, seasons 2012 to 2024
  • 233matches of the 2025-2026 season predicted

What sets it apart

A whole season predicted before kick-off

The stakes. Anticipating the balance of power in every match of a season before it starts, from what is known in August: squad values, the clubs’ historical hierarchy, their form at the end of the previous season. It is a planning question, for a club’s budget or a broadcaster’s schedule.

The usual approach

Predict match by match, feeding in the current season’s standings and form as the season goes.

Where it breaks. Those inputs do not exist in August. And a rolling statistic that sees the match it is meant to predict makes a model look great while predicting nothing.

What we did differently

  • Every feature rebuilt as it was known before the match: form, momentum and head-to-head only use earlier games.
  • Recent seasons weigh more than old ones, because they describe today’s league better.
  • Five hypotheses tested in the data before becoming features. Finding: squad value weighs more than recent form.

The proof

48.0%

Accuracy in cross-validation on three possible outcomes, against 34.1% for a random draw with the historical frequencies; the selected model is also the most stable of the three from one fold to the next.

What it does not do. The predictions are not yet scored on the real 2025-2026 season, nor compared with bookmaker odds. The cross-validation is not strictly forward in time and the historical rank leaks slightly: both are listed under Limits rather than hidden.

What it shows. Framing a prediction problem by what is actually known when the decision is made, and saying plainly what is not proven yet.

The problem

A three-class classification: 1 for a home win, 0 for a draw, −1 for an away win. The whole 2025-2026 season has to be predicted at once, so no match can use information from the matches played before it in that season: only what was known when the history ends.

The real difficulty is the time series. A pipeline that lets a rolling statistic see the match it is meant to predict would look great and predict nothing, so every rolling feature is shifted to use only earlier matches. Two milder leaks remain; they are listed under Limits.

The data

Domestic league matches from 2012 to 2024, yearly player market values taken before each season, and in-game events (goals, assists, cards). The exploratory analysis tested five hypotheses, and each one shaped a feature:

Does playing at home matter?44% home wins, 26% draws, 30% away winsBalanced class weights, more weight on recent seasons
Does squad value predict the result?Home win rate is 63% when the home squad is worth over 2× the visitor, and 24% under 0.5×Log of the value ratio, to tame PSG’s outlier values
Does the ranking gap matter?Home win rate climbs from 8% to 85% across the gap range, but the current ranking is unknown for a future seasonGap between the historical average final positions
Does recent form matter?58% home wins after 13 to 15 points in 5 matches, 32% after 3 points or fewerHome/away form and win-rate momentum over 5 matches
Does club status matter?PSG wins 76% of its home matches, the bulk of the league sits between 35% and 45%Cumulative win rate (target encoding), head-to-head
Complementary signals

Ranking, finances and form are weakly correlated with each other, and none of them correlates with the result above 0.4. There is no miracle variable: the prediction comes from combining them, which suits ensemble models.

Approach

  1. Build the context before kick-off. Squad value (total, average, log ratio), historical position gap, win-rate target encoding, home/away form over the last 5 matches, momentum, head-to-head goal difference, and rolling averages of assists, yellow cards, penalties and headers.
  2. Shift every rolling statistic. Form, momentum, head-to-head and target encoding only use matches strictly before the one being described.
  3. Weight the seasons. Each match gets the weight 0.75(latest season − its season), normalised to a mean of 1, because recent seasons describe today’s Ligue 1 better.
  4. Compare three models in scikit-learn pipelines with median imputation: a random forest, a gradient boosting and a logistic regression, against a stratified random baseline.
  5. Predict 2025-2026 from the latest known state of each club: last form and momentum, last squad value, last win rate, last head-to-head.

Results

Gradient boosting was kept: it has the highest mean and by far the most stable score across folds. The importance plot in the notebook puts the financial indicators first, then the club’s historical status, with recent form and game profile as adjustment variables: structural strength weighs more than short-term dynamics.

The 2025-2026 predictions

The 233 matches are those of the file provided by the course (a full 18-club season has 306). The model predicts 92 home wins, 76 away wins and 65 draws, so it does not fall back on always picking the home team. Its average confidence is about 51%, with most predictions between 35% and 50% and a long tail up to 90% for extreme mismatches, such as a top club against a bottom-table side.

Limits

Not scored yet

The 2025-2026 season ended in May 2026, but the 233 predictions have not yet been compared with the real results: the 48% is a cross-validation figure on past seasons. Scoring them, and comparing them with bookmaker odds, is the next step.

  • The cross-validation is not strictly forward in time. StratifiedKFold(shuffle=False) keeps matches in order inside the folds, but the early folds are predicted by models trained on later seasons. A walk-forward split would be the honest test.
  • The historical position is a mild leak. The average final rank is computed over all seasons, including the one a training match belongs to.
  • The 2025-2026 context is frozen. Form, momentum and win rates are the last known values and are not updated as the season goes on. A club with no history, such as a promoted one, falls back on the median imputation.
  • Class balancing is inconsistent. The random forest and the logistic regression use balanced class weights, the selected gradient boosting does not.
  • No outside reference. There is no comparison with bookmaker odds, the usual yardstick for football forecasts, and draws remain the hardest outcome to call.
