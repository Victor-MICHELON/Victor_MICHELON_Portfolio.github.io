Predicting Ligue 1 results, season 2025-2026

Predict the outcome of every Ligue 1 match of the 2025-2026 season (home win, draw or away win) before it is played. I built a leak-free pipeline that turns squad values, club hierarchy, recent form, head-to-head and in-game events into 23 features, then compared three models. The best one reaches 48.0% accuracy in cross-validation, against 34.1% for a random baseline.

  • 48.0%cross-validated accuracy of the gradient boosting model, on three possible outcomes
  • +14 ptsover a baseline that guesses at random with the historical class frequencies (34.1%)
  • 4,691historical matches used for training, seasons 2012 to 2024
  • 233matches of the 2025-2026 season predicted

The problem

A three-class classification: 1 for a home win, 0 for a draw, −1 for an away win. The whole 2025-2026 season has to be predicted at once, so no match can use information from the matches played before it in that season: only what was known when the history ends.

The real difficulty is the time series. A pipeline that lets a rolling statistic see the match it is meant to predict would look great and predict nothing, so every feature is built to avoid data leakage.

The data

Domestic league matches from 2012 to 2024, yearly player market values taken before each season, and in-game events (goals, assists, cards). The exploratory analysis tested five hypotheses, and each one shaped a feature:

Complementary signals

Ranking, finances and form are weakly correlated with each other, and none of them correlates with the result above 0.4. There is no miracle variable: the prediction comes from combining them, which suits ensemble models.

Approach

  1. Build the context before kick-off. Squad value (total, average, log ratio), historical position gap, win-rate target encoding, home/away form over the last 5 matches, momentum, head-to-head goal difference, and rolling averages of assists, yellow cards, penalties and headers.
  2. Shift every rolling statistic. Form, momentum, head-to-head and target encoding only use matches strictly before the one being described.
  3. Weight the seasons. Each match gets the weight 0.75(latest season − its season), normalised to a mean of 1, because recent seasons describe today’s Ligue 1 better.
  4. Compare three models in scikit-learn pipelines with median imputation: a random forest, a gradient boosting and a logistic regression, against a stratified random baseline.
  5. Predict 2025-2026 from the latest known state of each club: last form and momentum, last squad value, last win rate, last head-to-head.

Results

Gradient boosting was kept: it has the highest mean and by far the most stable score across folds. The importance plot in the notebook puts the financial indicators first, then the club’s historical status, with recent form and game profile as adjustment variables: structural strength weighs more than short-term dynamics.

The 2025-2026 predictions

The model predicts 92 home wins, 76 away wins and 65 draws over the 233 matches, so it does not fall back on always picking the home team. Its average confidence is about 51%, with most predictions between 35% and 50% and a long tail up to 90% for extreme mismatches, such as a top club against a bottom-table side.

Limits

Not scored yet

The 2025-2026 predictions are not compared with actual results anywhere in the notebook. The 48% is a cross-validation figure on the historical data, not a measured score on the predicted season.

  • The cross-validation is not strictly forward in time. StratifiedKFold(shuffle=False) keeps matches in order inside the folds, but the early folds are predicted by models trained on later seasons. A walk-forward split would be the honest test.
  • The historical position is a mild leak. The average final rank is computed over all seasons, including the one a training match belongs to.
  • The 2025-2026 context is frozen. Form, momentum and win rates are the last known values and are not updated as the season goes on. A club with no history, such as a promoted one, falls back on the median imputation.
  • Class balancing is inconsistent. The random forest and the logistic regression use balanced class weights, the selected gradient boosting does not.
  • No outside reference. There is no comparison with bookmaker odds, the usual yardstick for football forecasts, and draws remain the hardest outcome to call.
