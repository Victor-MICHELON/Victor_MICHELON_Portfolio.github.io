Recovering the true OFF score in Parkinson's disease

Bobathon ESILV with Probabl and IBM, October 2026

For each patient visit, predict the debiased OFF motor score (MDS-UPDRS) from the observed ON and OFF measurements, the levodopa dose, the time since intake and the patient's history. The synthetic training set has 44,590 visits from 5,576 patients, with many missing values: the OFF score is missing in 42% of visits, the dose in 37%.

I ran it as a controlled lab driven by Bob, and the chart below plots every run I logged. Every idea was a separate experiment, scored under one locked protocol (5 folds grouped by patient) and compared fold by fold with a reference run; a gain smaller than its spread across folds counted as noise. What worked: modelling the generating process (drug response curve, per-patient amplitude, per-rater scale), a latent trajectory per patient, sequence networks over each patient's visits, and an NNLS blend of five decorrelated models. The error fell from 16.51 to 2.51, an 85% reduction.

Limits. The data is synthetic and the scores are out-of-fold cross-validation, grouped by patient. The public leaderboard was only used to check that it tracked cross-validation, and I do not claim a rank here. Several ideas brought no gain above noise: fine per-rater bias, per-patient stacking, pseudo-labeling, heavy network tuning.

Python, scikit-learn and LightGBM gradient boosting, PyTorch (BiGRU, Transformer), pandas, skore, Bob agents for brainstorming, experiments and leakage review

CineMatch, a hybrid movie recommender

Streamlit app, 2025 to 2026

Pick a film and get similar ones, with the reasons. Eight similarity signals are blended: plot embeddings (Sentence-BERT, all-MiniLM-L6-v2), keywords, mood, genres, cast and director, origin, popularity and era, and an audience signal from MovieLens ratings.

The weights adapt to the source film through simple, readable rules: horror leans on mood, animation and sequels on topics and audience. 43% of the 6,203 films have no audience data, so their audience weight goes to the content signals instead of being wasted. A radar chart shows the exact weights behind each result. Everything is pre-computed, so a query is a weighted sum of eight matrix rows, about 0.2 ms on a laptop, and the engine is separate from the UI and unit-tested.

Limits. There is no offline evaluation yet, so quality is judged by inspection; precision@k and NDCG on held-out MovieLens ratings come next. Two weighting rules, for non-English and very popular films, are inactive because the shipped data lacks their columns, and the scripts behind two of the eight signals are not in the repository yet.

Python, sentence-transformers, NumPy, pandas, Plotly, Streamlit, TMDB and MovieLens data

Brain tumor classification with Grad-CAM

Deep learning on MRI, December 2025

Four classes: glioma, meningioma, pituitary tumor and no tumor, with 5,712 MRI scans for training and 1,311 for testing. A CNN trained from scratch is the baseline for a fine-tuned EfficientNetB0 pretrained on ImageNet. Grad-CAM overlays show which part of the scan drove each prediction.

On the held-out test set, the fine-tuned model reaches 97.8% accuracy (macro F1 0.98) against 92.1% (0.92) for the baseline, and the recall on its weakest class, meningioma, rises from 0.79 to 0.94. A reproducible src/ package goes further: near-duplicate detection by perceptual hash for a leak-free split, the same training budget for both models, bootstrap confidence intervals, calibration, and a quantitative check of Grad-CAM against the tumor masks.

Limits. Validation accuracy (99.9%) is well above test accuracy (97.8%), a sign of possible train and validation leakage. The src/ pipeline measures it but has not been run on the real data yet, so the figures still come from the notebook. One seed (about ±0.8 point on accuracy), no external validation set, and the Grad-CAM maps are qualitative evidence only. This is an educational project, not a diagnostic tool.

Python, TensorFlow, Keras (EfficientNetB0), scikit-learn, Grad-CAM implemented by hand

Tsunami risk from earthquake data

Classification on 782 earthquakes, October 2025

Can the seismic profile of an earthquake alone say whether it will cause a tsunami? The dataset has 782 significant earthquakes from 2001 to 2022, with 13 seismic attributes. I compared eight models with repeated stratified cross-validation (10 folds, 3 repeats) inside leak-proof pipelines, tuned the two best, and gradient boosting won.

Two decisions matter most. Every record before 2012 was a negative case, so the year alone would have leaked the answer: I removed those rows, which leaves 449 events. I also added a physical feature, the distance from the epicenter to the nearest tectonic plate boundary, computed with GeoPandas. The model is tuned for recall, because missing a real tsunami costs far more than a false alarm: it detects 95% of tsunamis, with an F1 of 0.90 in line with cross-validation (0.905).

Limits. The hold-out set has only 90 events, 61 of them tsunamis. A model that always answered "tsunami" would reach a tsunami F1 of 0.81 on that split, so read 0.90 with that in mind. Recall on non-tsunami events is 0.66, the price of the recall-first choice.

Python, scikit-learn (Pipeline, GridSearchCV), GeoPandas, Shapely, pyproj, Seaborn
